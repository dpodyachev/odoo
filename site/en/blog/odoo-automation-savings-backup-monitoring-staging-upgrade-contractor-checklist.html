Automating Odoo operations: what each step saves – and what to demand from your contractor

Backups with automatic restore tests, monitoring, staging copies on command, update and upgrade pipelines, industry templates, training material: twelve automations for an Odoo project on your own server – each with the hours and euros it saves per year and why. At the end: what a customer should demand from the contractor to lower the budget and raise the quality, and how to check it.

In our cost comparison, Odoo on your own server lost to Odoo.sh over five years – not because of the server, but because of the hours around it: administration, support cases, staging copies, version upgrades. Odoo.sh is cheaper because a platform does this work. The same can be built for your own server. This article goes through it step by step and puts a price on each step.

The numbers use the model from our Odoo vs SAP comparison: 5 users, Odoo Community on your own server, 90 € per hour, 1,000 € per day. They are estimates for planning, not measurements – the calculation is at the end so you can insert your own values.

The starting point: what costs time without automation

  • administration (updates, backups, monitoring): 24 hours a year;
  • support cases ("mail does not work", "bot is silent", "error after update"): 16 hours a year;
  • server setup ½ day, implementation 8 days, training 2 days, then ½ day a year;
  • a new Odoo version every 2–3 years: 5 days with OpenUpgrade;
  • over 5 years: 37,300 € – more than Odoo.sh (35,670 €).

1. Server as code

One script or playbook creates the whole server: VPS via the provider's API, DNS, Docker Compose with Odoo and PostgreSQL, nginx with HTTPS, firewall, backups and monitoring. The same files build production, staging and a replacement server.

  • Time: ½ day → 1 hour per server; a replacement server after a total failure in about an hour instead of a day.
  • Money: about 400 € per server, more in an emergency – the hour of downtime of the whole company is the expensive part.
  • Why: Manual setup is never done the same way twice; the forgotten firewall rule or backup job shows up months later. A script is also the documentation.

2. Backups with automatic restore test

Every night: database dump and filestore to a second location (e.g. S3/R2). Every night or every week: the newest backup is restored into a throw-away container, a few figures are compared, the result goes to the admin chat.

  • Time: about 6 hours a year of manual checks and test restores.
  • Money: about 540 € a year.
  • Why: A backup that has never been restored is a hope, not a backup. The automatic test also tells you how long a restore takes – the number you need in an emergency.

3. Monitoring and alarms

A bot checks every few minutes: Odoo answers, disk space, certificate expiry, mail queue, failed cron jobs, errors in the log, backup age – and writes to the admin chat before a user calls.

  • Time: about 5 hours a year (2 admin, 3 support).
  • Money: about 450 € a year, plus downtime avoided – 5 people unable to work for an hour cost more than the bot.
  • Why: Most outages announce themselves: the disk fills over weeks, the certificate expires on a known date. Without monitoring, the user finds the problem – with monitoring, the admin does, usually before it matters.

4. Staging copy on command

One command copies production into a test database and neutralises it: outgoing mail, cron jobs, payment providers and integrations are switched off, a "TEST" banner appears.

  • Time: 2–3 hours → 10 minutes per copy; about 3 hours a year.
  • Money: about 270 € a year – and no test e-mails to real customers.
  • Why: Updates and new modules should be tried on real data. If a copy takes half a day, people test on production. Without neutralisation the copy sends reminders to customers and duplicates bot messages.

5. Update pipeline within a version

Once a week: the newest image of the same Odoo version is installed on staging, tests run, then the image goes to production in a maintenance window; the previous image and a database snapshot stay for a rollback.

  • Time: about 5 hours a year.
  • Money: about 450 € a year.
  • Why: Security fixes arrive every week. Manually they are postponed – and then many changes come at once, which is riskier. With a rollback, an update is no longer an adventure.

6. Tests and CI for your own modules

Every change to your own modules runs automatically: installation on an empty database, tests of the critical flows (quote → invoice, stock move, DATEV export), code checks – on the current and on the next Odoo version.

  • Time: about 2 hours a year of support for errors that tests would have caught; more for larger customisations.
  • Money: about 180 € a year, and the upgrade becomes cheaper (next section).
  • Why: An error found in CI costs minutes; the same error found by the accountant at month end costs hours and nerves.

7. Upgrade pipeline to the new Odoo version

Enterprise: the database copy goes through Odoo's upgrade service; Community: OpenUpgrade in a container. Then your modules are built and tested on the new version, all on staging. The result is a list of what breaks – long before the actual upgrade day.

  • Time: 5 days → 2 days per upgrade.
  • Money: about 3,000 € per upgrade, every 2–3 years.
  • Why: Odoo.sh is cheap here because the platform does exactly this. Most of the work in an upgrade is finding out what breaks; the pipeline does that automatically and every week.

8. Industry templates instead of starting from zero

Settings as a data module: chart of accounts (SKR03/SKR04), taxes, DATEV, document layouts, e-mail templates, typical products and work centres – one template per industry (craft business, wholesale, shop with till).

  • Time: implementation 8 → 6 days.
  • Money: about 2,000 € per project.
  • Why: Two thirds of every implementation are the same in every company of the industry. A tested template is also correct from the first day – no forgotten tax setting.

9. Training material: videos, checklists, texts

Short videos (2–5 minutes) of your own processes with transcript in your language, checklists for typical problems, one page with all links.

  • Time: training 2 → 1 day; refresher ½ → ¼ day a year; new employees learn alone.
  • Money: about 1,000 € at the start, 250 € a year after that.
  • Why: A trainer explains the same five processes to every new person. A video does this at night, and the transcript makes it searchable.

10. Checklists, self-service and standard mail setup

For "mail does not work", "bot is silent", "does not print": checklists for users and admins, bot commands for the customer's admin (status, mail queue, restart), mail set up the same way everywhere with SPF/DKIM/DMARC checks.

  • Time: about 3 hours a year of support.
  • Money: about 270 € a year.
  • Why: Half of all support cases are the same five problems. A checklist solves them without a ticket, or turns a vague "it does not work" into a ticket with the right details.

11. Access, secrets and documentation

Personal accounts with 2FA instead of a shared admin, secrets in a password manager or secret store – not in the repository, an admin guide in the repository: where what runs, how to restore, whom to call.

  • Time: a new admin gets access in 15 minutes instead of 2 hours; a change of contractor takes 1 day instead of 3–5.
  • Money: hard to price per year; at a change of contractor 2,000–4,000 €.
  • Why: Without documentation the customer depends on one person. Documentation and clean access make the contractor replaceable – which also keeps prices honest.

12. Several small customers on one server (optional)

Small installations share a larger server, each with its own database, domain and backup; monitoring and updates run once for all.

  • Time: administration per customer drops further.
  • Money: hosting about 360 → 150 € a year per customer.
  • Why: This is how Odoo.sh works with its shared workers. The price: less isolation – a customer with heavy reports can slow the others; for sensitive data a separate server remains the better choice.

What a customer should demand from the contractor – and how to check it

Most of these points cost the contractor little once they are set up – and they decide whether you pay for hours or for results. Put them in the contract and check them, not just once:

DemandHow to check
Everything in a Git repository that belongs to you – modules, configuration, Docker files, scripts.You have owner rights in the repository; ask for a deployment to be done from it while you watch. → step by step
Reproducible server (server as code).Ask for a fresh staging server to be built from the repository – and time it. → step by step
Backups in a second location and an automatic restore test with a report.Ask for the last restore reports; name a random date and ask for that backup to be restored on staging. → step by step
Monitoring with alarms, and you are in the alarm chat.You see the alarms yourself; ask for the list of last month's alarms and what was done. → step by step
A neutralised staging copy before every update.Send yourself a test mail from staging – it must not leave the system. → step by step
Tests and CI for every own module, merged only when green.Look at the pipeline: green builds, tests for the critical flows (quote → invoice, DATEV export). → step by step
As little own code as possible: standard or OCA first, a reason for every own module.List of own modules with reason and size; question every module that only renames fields. → step by step
An upgrade plan: which version when, what breaks, how much it costs.Ask for the test upgrade of a copy to the next version and its report. → step by step
Fixed scope with acceptance criteria instead of open hours.Each item has a written test case; you sign off item by item. → step by step
Documentation and handover: admin guide, access, contacts.Let a third person (or another contractor) restore a backup using only the guide. → step by step
Personal accounts with 2FA, no secrets in the repository.List of accounts and who has them; a secret scan of the repository (e.g. gitleaks). → step by step
Training material in your language: videos, checklists.A new employee does a typical task using only the material. → step by step
Reaction times and a monthly report of hours by category (support, admin, development).Compare the categories quarter by quarter – automation must show up as falling support and admin hours. → step by step
Exit clause: data export and handover at the end of the contract.Do a trial export once: database, filestore, repository – can someone else start it? → step by step

Conclusion

Odoo on your own server is only expensive when people do what scripts can do. Twelve automations cost a contractor 15–25 days once and save a small company about half of the running costs – over five years about 18,000 €, so that your own server ends below Odoo.sh. As a customer you do not need to understand every script: it is enough to demand the results listed above and check them regularly.
