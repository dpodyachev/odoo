SAP ECC ends in 2027: S/4HANA, extended maintenance or Odoo? A decision guide for mid-size companies

SAP's mainstream maintenance for ECC 6.0 ends on 31 December 2027. The three real options – S/4HANA, staying on ECC with extended or third-party maintenance, or a move to another ERP such as Odoo – with an honest criteria table, where Odoo is better and where SAP stays better, what a move from ECC to Odoo involves, and a small scoring script.

Many mid-size companies have run SAP ECC 6.0 for fifteen years or more. It works, people know it, and the custom ABAP programs do exactly what the business needs. But the maintenance clock is running out – and the question in many management meetings is no longer whether to move, but where to. This article does not repeat price tables (they are in our Odoo vs SAP cost comparison); it is about the decision.

Dates as of 25 November 2026, from SAP's support strategy page and SAP Community; SAP's binding terms are in SAP Note 2881788 and your contract. We are an independent Odoo service provider, not an Odoo partner and not an SAP partner. SAP, ECC and S/4HANA are trademarks of SAP SE.

  • 2027 is not a switch-off date. ECC keeps running. What stops is new legal changes (tax, payroll, e-invoicing formats) and new fixes – which is exactly what a German company needs every year.
  • Extended maintenance buys time, not a solution. It costs more each year and ends in 2030.

The three real options

  1. S/4HANA – on-premise or as a subscription (RISE with SAP for existing customers, GROW with SAP for new ones). A conversion (brownfield) keeps much of your configuration and custom code; a new implementation (greenfield) cleans up but costs like a new ERP. SAP supports S/4HANA much longer, which is its biggest argument.
  2. Stay on ECC – with extended maintenance until 2030, or with a third-party maintenance provider. Sensible when a decision is still open or a merger is coming. Risk: every year without a decision makes the later project bigger, and the SAP specialists who know ECC get rarer and more expensive.
  3. Move to another ERP – for example Odoo. It means a real migration project: new processes, new data model, new training. In return the licence and operating costs are usually much lower and the system is easier to change yourself.

If most of your answers fall in the right column, S/4HANA is very likely the more sensible choice – and that is fine. If most fall in the left one, Odoo deserves a serious fit-gap check. The script below turns this into a rough score you can adjust.

Where Odoo is genuinely better

  • Cost of ownership: licences per user are a fraction of SAP's, and Community even costs nothing – numbers in our cost comparison.
  • One database, one interface: CRM, sales, purchase, stock, manufacturing, accounting, website and helpdesk in one system – no separate CRM or shop to integrate.
  • Speed of change: a new field, report or automation is often a matter of hours, and Python developers are easier to find than ABAP developers.
  • Ownership: the source code (Community, and your own modules) is yours; you can run it on your own server in Germany.

Where SAP stays better

  • Large groups and complex consolidation across many countries and currencies.
  • Deep industry solutions and certified processes that customers or auditors demand.
  • An ecosystem you already use: if BW, SuccessFactors or Ariba are central, leaving SAP's core is costly.
  • Available consultants for large projects: for a multi-country rollout, SAP's partner market is much bigger.

What a move from ECC to Odoo involves

  1. Inventory: which ECC transactions, reports and custom programs are really used? Usage statistics (transaction ST03N) help – often a large part of custom code is no longer used.
  2. Data extraction: master data (customers, vendors, materials, BOMs, routings), open items, opening balances and open orders – exported from ECC tables or via standard reports, cleaned and mapped to Odoo's model. History stays in ECC as a read-only archive.
  3. Rethink, don't port, ABAP: a custom program was usually a workaround for a process. Ask what the process needs; often Odoo's standard or a small module covers it.
  4. Interfaces: list every system that talks to SAP (EDI, bank, shop, logistics) and plan its Odoo counterpart.
  5. Cut-over: rehearsals on a copy, a frozen weekend, reconciliation, rollback criteria – step by step in our article on switching from an old ERP.

Decision checklist

  1. Find out your enhancement package and your maintenance end date – EHP 0–5 is already outside mainstream maintenance.
  2. Ask SAP or your partner for two written offers: S/4HANA conversion and extended maintenance until 2030.
  3. Count the custom programs that are used – not the ones that exist.
  4. Pick three real processes (for example order to cash, purchase to pay, a production order) and test them in Odoo on a demo database.
  5. Compare five-year costs for all options, including internal effort – not just licences.
  6. Decide by mid-2027 at the latest: a mid-size move takes months, and the decision must come before the deadline, not with it.

Conclusion

The end of ECC's mainstream maintenance forces a decision, but not automatically S/4HANA. Large groups with deep SAP landscapes will usually stay with SAP. Mid-size companies with simple integrations and a cost problem should at least test Odoo seriously – three processes and a five-year cost comparison are enough for a sound decision.
