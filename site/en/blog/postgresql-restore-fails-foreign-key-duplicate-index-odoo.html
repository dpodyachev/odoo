The backup exists – but will not restore: foreign keys, duplicates in unique indexes, missing indexes

Why a pg_dump that ran without errors can fail on pg_restore, how to see every error at once, how to find duplicates and orphaned rows, why a corrupted index is often the real cause – and a few SQL patterns from real repairs.

The nightly pg_dump ran without a single error for months. Then the day comes when you need it – and pg_restore stops with could not create unique index or violates foreign key constraint. The data in the dump is exactly what was in the database. So the database itself was already broken.

Short version: pg_dump copies data, it does not check it. Indexes and foreign keys are only rebuilt on restore – and that is where hidden inconsistencies surface. Restore regularly as a test, check the source with pg_amcheck, and read every line of the restore log.

Typical errors

  • Duplicate key in a unique index – the source database contains duplicates that its (corrupted) index no longer noticed.
  • Foreign key violated – child rows point to parents that no longer exist (deleted by hand, a failed migration, a constraint dropped at some point).
  • Role does not exist – the dump carries owners and grants: restore with --no-owner --no-privileges.
  • Index does not exist with --clean on an empty database – harmless; use --clean --if-exists.
  • Unsupported version in file header – the dump was made by a newer pg_dump; restore with a pg_restore of at least that version.

Why pg_restore still says “done”

By default pg_restore continues after errors and ends with errors ignored on restore: N. Tables and data are there – only the failed index or foreign key is missing. Odoo starts, looks normal, and the database has no uniqueness or referential checks where they failed. That is worse than a clear abort.

Duplicates: often the index was corrupted, not the application

A unique index cannot let duplicates in – unless it is corrupted. The classic cause: an operating-system upgrade with a new glibc (2.28 changed the sort order of text). Indexes on text columns built under the old sort order are then silently wrong; inserts slip past them. pg_dump does not notice, the restore builds the index fresh – and fails.

  • Clean up the duplicates in the source (merge or delete the wrong row – for Odoo records carefully, other tables may reference them), then REINDEX the affected indexes and dump again.
  • After every OS upgrade under PostgreSQL: REINDEX the text indexes (PostgreSQL 15+ also warns about a collation version mismatch).

Prevention

  • Test restores regularly (stage, weekly or monthly) and check the restore log for ERROR – automatically, not by eye.
  • pg_amcheck on the production database now and then, and after every OS/glibc upgrade a REINDEX.
  • pg_dump and pg_restore of the same major version as the server, or pg_restore newer than pg_dump.
  • Never drop constraints “temporarily” and forget them; never delete rows by hand without checking who references them.
  • Keep more than one generation of backups – the damage is often older than the last dump.
