Restoring a backup on stage – without cron jobs and queue jobs firing

Why stage systems can send dunning letters to real customers, and the order of steps that prevents it: SQL before the first start, neutralize, a stage config.

The production database is restored on stage to test an update. Odoo starts – and immediately processes everything that became "due" during the downtime: dunning and invoice e-mails, syncs with the shop, bank or carriers, queued jobs. With real customer data and real credentials. The classic stage accident.

The core point: Odoo starts its cron threads and the job runner right at startup. Switching things off in the UI is too late. Everything has to happen before Odoo sees the database for the first time.

1. Restore – Odoo stays stopped

2. Neutralise with SQL

The tables queue_job and fetchmail_server only exist when the modules are installed – drop those lines otherwise. The zz_stage_cron_before list helps to re-enable individual crons on stage later on purpose.

Since Odoo 16 there is also the built-in neutralize command: among other things it deactivates scheduled actions, mail servers and payment providers and shows a "neutralized" banner. I use it as the base and add the SQL for third-party modules such as queue_job.

3. A stage config as a second safety net

Even if a database is restored without neutralising, nothing can leave the server with this config: no cron thread, no job runner, e-mail only in the mail catcher.

4. Verify

  • Release stage for testing only when all three queries look right.
  • Replace credentials for external services (shop, bank, shipping, payments) on stage with sandbox keys, or remove them.
  • Keep the whole procedure as a script – a fresh stage is then a matter of minutes, and no step is forgotten.
