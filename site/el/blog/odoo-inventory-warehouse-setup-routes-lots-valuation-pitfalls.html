Odoo Inventory: the setup decisions you cannot easily undo – routes, lots, valuation, opening stock

Το άρθρο δεν είναι ακόμη διαθέσιμο στα ελληνικά – ακολουθεί η αγγλική έκδοση.

What Odoo Inventory covers in Community and Enterprise, which settings are hard to change once goods move (steps, routes, lots and serial numbers, units of measure, costing method), how to bring opening stock over from an old ERP, where warehouses get slow – and a checklist with a read-only SQL check.

Most Odoo warehouse problems we are called for are not bugs. They are decisions made in the first week – often by clicking through the settings – that become expensive once thousands of moves exist. This article is about those decisions, the move of opening stock from an old system, and the technical side that decides whether the warehouse stays fast.

Menus and behaviour as of Odoo 17–19. We are an independent Odoo service provider, not an Odoo partner. Test every change on a copy of the database first.

What Odoo Inventory covers – Community and Enterprise

  • Community (free licence): warehouses and locations, receipts, deliveries and internal transfers, routes and reordering rules, lots and serial numbers, packages, units of measure, physical inventory, stock valuation methods, Purchase, Sales and Manufacturing (bills of materials, manufacturing orders).
  • Enterprise only: the Barcode app for scanners, Quality, PLM, the Master Production Schedule, shipping-carrier connectors and the full Accounting app. Barcode is often the real reason a warehouse needs Enterprise.
  • Integration: everything is in one database, so a sales order, its reservation, the delivery and the invoice refer to the same records – no nightly sync between a WMS and an ERP.

Decisions that are hard to change later

DecisionWhereChanging it later
Steps for receipts and deliveries (1, 2 or 3)Inventory → Configuration → Warehousespossible, but open transfers keep the old route – change it when nothing is in flight
Multi-step routes, several warehousesSettings → Storage Locations / Multi-Step Routesnew rules apply to new moves only; reports mix old and new flows
Lots and serial numbers per productSettings → Lots & Serial Numbers, product → Trackingstock already on hand has no lot – you have to count it again with lots
Unit of measure of a productproduct formblocked once the product has stock moves – a new product is the usual way out
Costing method (Standard, AVCO, FIFO)product categoryrevalues the whole stock of the category on the change date
Automated (perpetual) valuationproduct category → Inventory Valuationevery move then creates journal entries; switching mid-year confuses the books
  • Start simple. One-step receipts and deliveries are enough for most small warehouses. Two or three steps make sense when different people pick, pack and ship – not because the option exists.
  • Decide on lots and serials before the first receipt. Food, chemicals and anything with an expiry date: lots. Devices with warranty: serial numbers.
  • Costing method with your tax adviser. AVCO is the common choice in Germany; FIFO when prices change a lot and you must prove the value per receipt. Automated valuation is only useful when the accounting side (stock accounts per category) is set up – and the full Accounting app is Enterprise.

A typical flow in one database

  1. A purchase order is confirmed – Odoo creates a receipt with the expected quantities.
  2. The receipt is validated – the quantities become quants (stock per product, location and lot) and, with valuation, a valuation layer with the purchase price.
  3. A sales order is confirmed – the delivery reserves stock according to the Reservation Method of the operation type (at confirmation, manually, or a number of days before the scheduled date).
  4. The delivery is validated – quants go down, the invoice can be created from the delivered quantities.
  5. Manufacturing consumes components from a bill of materials and produces the finished product; missing components are bought or made by reordering rules, which the nightly scheduler processes.

Opening stock from the old ERP

  • Products first, then quantities. Import products with internal reference, unit of measure, category (= costing method) and cost. With AVCO and FIFO the opening quantity is valued at the cost the product has at that moment.
  • Quantities via Physical Inventory. Inventory → Operations → Physical Inventory: import a file with product, location, lot (if tracked) and counted quantity, check the differences, then Apply. Each line becomes a move from the inventory-adjustment location – traceable later.
  • Cut-over date. Count or export the stock when nothing moves (evening, weekend), and make sure the accounting date of the adjustment is the cut-over date, so the opening value lands in the right period.
  • Open documents separately. Open purchase and sales orders are re-entered or imported as new orders, not as stock. Never import past moves just to have history – keep the old system read-only for that.
  • Rehearse once. Run the whole import on a copy, compare totals per location and the stock value with the old system, then repeat on the live database.

Shop and accounting

An Odoo webshop reads the same quants, so availability is correct without a sync. External shops (Shopware, WooCommerce, marketplaces) need a connector – plan who owns the stock figure and how often it is pushed. On the accounting side the stock value and the journal entries go to the tax adviser; for Germany see our DATEV export guide.

Where warehouses get slow

  • Tables grow forever. stock_move, stock_move_line and stock_valuation_layer only grow; moves are never archived. After a few years, reservations, the forecast and valuation reports read millions of rows.
  • Too many quants. Many locations, lots and packages per product multiply the quants. Stock levels in list views are computed over them – a product list with thousands of items and many locations can take seconds.
  • The scheduler. The run scheduler cron (procurement.group.run_scheduler) recomputes reordering rules and tries to reserve waiting moves. Run it at night, not every few minutes, and give it enough time limit.
  • Concurrency. Several people validating transfers of the same products at the same moment cause serialization errors; workers and short transactions help, see how many users one server handles.
  • Database care. Regular VACUUM ANALYZE, enough memory for PostgreSQL and a look at slow queries keep stock screens fast long before new hardware is needed.

Checklist

  1. Write down the real flow: who receives, who picks, who packs, who ships.
  2. Choose steps and routes for that flow – as few as possible.
  3. Decide lots or serial numbers per product category.
  4. Fix units of measure and purchase units before the first move.
  5. Choose the costing method and valuation with your tax adviser.
  6. Rehearse the opening-stock import on a copy and reconcile quantities and value.
  7. Schedule the scheduler at night and watch the size of the stock tables once a quarter.

Conclusion

Odoo Inventory covers a typical warehouse with purchase, sales and production in one database – in Community already, with Barcode as the main Enterprise argument. What makes it work is not the number of features switched on but a few decisions made deliberately before the first receipt, a rehearsed opening-stock import and a database that is looked after.
